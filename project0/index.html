Becoming Friends
With Your Camera

An exploration of perspective, focal length, camera position, and the center of projection through portraiture, architectural photography, and the classic dolly zoom.

01 / PORTRAIT

Selfie: The Wrong Way
vs. The Right Way

The first pair compares a portrait photographed from very close range with another taken from farther away while zooming in enough to preserve approximately the same face size.

Close Distance

Close-up

Camera positioned very close to the subject.

Farther Distance

Zoomed In

Camera moved farther away and focal length increased.

Observation

In the close-up image, the camera is much nearer to the face, which exaggerates differences in depth between facial features. Features closer to the camera appear relatively larger. Moving farther away reduces these perspective differences, while zooming in restores a similar framing.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Architectural
Perspective Compression

This pair performs the inverse experiment on an architectural scene. One image is photographed from farther away using zoom, while the second is taken after moving closer and reducing the focal length.

Far Distance

Zoomed In

Photographed from farther away with a longer focal length.

Near Distance

Close-up

Camera moved closer while using a wider field of view.

Observation

From the distant camera position, differences in distance between near and far portions of the scene become small relative to the camera-to-scene distance. As a result, depth appears compressed. Moving closer increases relative depth differences and produces stronger perspective convergence.

03 / DOLLY ZOOM

The Vertigo Effect

For the final experiment, I moved the camera backward while simultaneously zooming in. The subject remains approximately constant in size while the background perspective changes dramatically.

Dolly Zoom · Final Animation Move Backward + Zoom In
Observation

The dolly zoom combines two operations that affect the image differently. Zoom controls framing, while camera translation changes perspective. By adjusting the focal length to offset the apparent change in subject size, the subject can remain stable while the background appears to expand or contract.
